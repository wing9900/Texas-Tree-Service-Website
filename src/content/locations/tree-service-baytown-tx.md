Baytown sits just across the Fred Hartman Bridge from our Pasadena shop, so your estimate and your crew both arrive fast. Every job in Baytown is priced free, run by an ISA Certified Arborist, and paid for only when the work is done and you are satisfied.

## Can you handle the big water oaks and pines in Baytown's older neighborhoods?

Yes, and they are the trees we see most in Baytown. The established streets around Wooster and Goose Creek grew their shade for fifty years or more, and neighborhoods named for it, Roseland Oaks and Country Club Oaks, are not exaggerating. A water oak that size does not come down in one piece. It comes down in sections, rigged and lowered, which is exactly what our [large tree removal](/services/tree-removal/large-tree-removal/) crews do week in and week out.

If the tree is dead, cracked, or leaning toward the house, treat it as a [hazardous tree removal](/services/tree-removal/hazardous-tree-removal/) and get it looked at before the next storm makes the decision for you.

## What do Gulf storms do to Baytown trees?

Baytown takes hurricane wind off the bay with less to slow it down than almost anywhere else we serve. The 500 acres at the Baytown Nature Center sit right in it, and the wooded trails at W.L. Jenkins Park tell the same story after every named storm. The [National Weather Service in Houston](https://www.weather.gov/hgx/) tracks these storms closely, and after each one, the calls from Baytown come in waves: snapped pines, split oaks, limbs through fences.

The cheaper option is the work done before the storm. [Storm preparation trimming](/services/tree-trimming/storm-preparation-trimming/) removes the dead wood and overextended limbs that fail first, and it costs a fraction of an emergency call.

The estimate is free, and the price is firm before we start. Cost depends on the size of the tree, what it hangs over, and how close equipment can get. You never pay a deposit, and every removal includes [stump grinding](/services/stump-grinding/) so you get a lawn back instead of a stump.

## Who decides whether a Baytown tree can come down?

You do, on your own property, in most cases, but two things sit outside that. Trees in a utility easement or the public right-of-way are handled differently in every city we work in, and anything growing into the conductors themselves belongs to [CenterPoint Energy](https://www.centerpointenergy.com/en-us/services/electricity/vegetation-management) vegetation management rather than to a private crew.

One thing worth correcting, because it circulates constantly: Texas did not ban city tree ordinances. The 2017 bills that would have done that never became law. What did pass limits mitigation fees, so a city cannot charge you one for removing a tree under 10 inches in diameter at your own one- or two-family home. Whether Baytown requires a permit at all is a city-specific question, and we confirm it for your address before the job is scheduled instead of assuming. None of this is legal advice.
