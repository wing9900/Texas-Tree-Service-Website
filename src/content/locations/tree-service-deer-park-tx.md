## When should Deer Park trees be trimmed for storm season?

Before June, when hurricane season starts in earnest. [Storm preparation trimming](/services/tree-trimming/storm-preparation-trimming/) takes out the dead wood, the cracked limbs, and the overextended branches over the roof, which are the pieces that fail first in wind. Walk the trails at Dow Park after any real blow and the same pattern is on the ground: the wood that came down was dead or cracked long before the storm arrived. The [National Weather Service Houston/Galveston office](https://www.weather.gov/hgx/) is the place to watch the forecasts; our job is making sure your trees are ready before those forecasts matter.

## Is regular pruning worth it for a healthy Deer Park tree?

Yes, because pruning done right is what keeps the tree healthy and the big bills away. Our [tree pruning](/services/tree-trimming/tree-pruning/) works from the ANSI A300 standard: cuts at the branch collar, no climbing spikes in a tree that is staying, and canopy removal kept conservative rather than drastic. The shade trees people single out at Deer Meadows Park, and the hardwoods along the boardwalk at the Deer Park Nature Preserve, got that way by growing with room and without damage, which is what good pruning is trying to imitate in a yard.

## When is it safe to prune an oak in Deer Park?

Outside the spring, and with every cut painted whatever the season. Oak wilt is confirmed in Harris County, and the fungus travels on beetles drawn to fresh wounds, so the risky window runs roughly February into June. We schedule Deer Park oak work outside it when the tree can wait, and we seal cuts on every oak year round, because that habit is what actually breaks the cycle. Texas A&M Forest Service publishes the current guidance at [tfsweb.tamu.edu](https://tfsweb.tamu.edu/).
