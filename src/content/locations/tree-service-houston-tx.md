Houston is a forest with a city in it, and the southeast side we serve is some of the oldest canopy in town. From our shop in Pasadena we run crews up SH 225 and I-45 into the East End, Glenbrook Valley, and the neighborhoods around Hobby Airport, where live oaks planted generations ago now stand over single-story roofs, driveways, and every wire on the block. Golfcrest and the South Belt corridor out toward Ellington run the same way.

## Can you remove a big tree from a tight Houston lot?

Yes, that is the job we are built for. Houston's older southeast neighborhoods mean narrow side yards, back fences with no gate access, and trees far too big to fell in one piece. Glenbrook Valley is the clearest case: mid-century houses on generous lots, with the original canopy now taller than anything else on the street. A [large tree removal](/services/tree-removal/large-tree-removal/) here is climbed, rigged, and lowered a section at a time, planned by an ISA Certified Arborist before anyone starts a saw. If the tree is already failing, dead, or storm-cracked, it needs the [hazardous tree removal](/services/tree-removal/hazardous-tree-removal/) treatment and a faster phone call.

## How should Houston live oaks be trimmed?

To the ANSI A300 standard, which is the written standard cities and utilities hold their own contractors to. Our [canopy trimming](/services/tree-trimming/canopy-trimming/) lifts the crown off your roof and clears the streetlights and sightlines, using reduction cuts back to sound laterals rather than topping. The Tree Care Industry Association, which publishes those standards, explains what professional tree care should look like at [tcia.org](https://www.tcia.org/).

## What about the mess a Houston storm leaves behind?

We haul it. After hurricanes and derecho winds, city pickup schedules stretch out for weeks, and a limb pile at the curb becomes a fixture. Our [debris removal](/services/land-clearing/debris-removal/) crews chip and haul storm wood off your property in one visit, and the [wood chipping](/services/land-clearing/wood-chipping/) can leave you mulch if you want it.

## Does Houston require a permit to take down your tree?

Usually not, for a tree in the back yard at an existing single-family home. Houston's Chapter 33 tree and shrub ordinance is built around development and the public right-of-way, and existing single-family residential property is not subject to its main requirements. A large tree in the front setback along a major street is the exception worth knowing about, since the ordinance can still classify it as a protected corridor tree by size alone.

The tree between the sidewalk and the street is a different animal. Removing one there takes written permission from the city's Urban Forestry Division, and the city's published guide cites penalties reaching $500 per day plus $90 per diameter inch for destroying a protected tree without it. That boundary is easy to misjudge from the driveway and expensive to get wrong, so we work out which side of it your tree sits on before we quote. New construction is the other trigger, since a new single-family home has to preserve or plant qualifying trees before final building inspection. The ordinance and the city's guide to it are posted at [houstontx.gov](https://www.houstontx.gov/planning/). None of this is legal advice and ordinances change, so we check it fresh per address.
