## How far is Tomball from our Conroe base?

About thirty minutes southwest, running FM 1488 across to SH 249. We cover the 77375 and 77377 ZIP codes, which is considerably larger than the city itself and takes in Pinehurst and the Decker Prairie area on the Montgomery County side.

Because the route runs through Magnolia, Tomball and Magnolia jobs get scheduled together. If you are booking non urgent work here, asking about our Magnolia days usually gets you on the calendar sooner.

## What kind of tree work does Tomball need?

Mature tree maintenance, more than removal. Tomball has a large stock of established homes on generous lots, and the trees on them are old enough to need real attention but healthy enough to be worth keeping.

That means [tree pruning](/services/tree-trimming/tree-pruning/) done properly: deadwood out, crossing limbs cleared, clearance from roofs and drives, and structural cuts that do not leave stubs. It is the least dramatic work we do and the work that prevents the most emergencies.

The second common job is roots. Post oaks and water oaks planted close to houses forty years ago now have root plates running under foundations, driveways, and sewer lines. [Root removal](/services/stump-grinding/root-removal/) and [root barrier installation](/services/tree-health/root-barrier-installation/) solve that without taking the tree, which is almost always the outcome the homeowner wanted in the first place.

## Which trees grow around Tomball?

Loblolly pine, post oak, water oak, and southern red oak, with sweetgum and yaupon through the understory. Burroughs Park northeast of downtown is 320 acres of exactly this mix, and it is a fair preview of what is standing in most established yards here.

Post oak deserves its own warning. It is the slowest to show stress and the least tolerant of disturbance in this whole region. Grade changes, trenching, and compaction from construction traffic kill post oaks years after the work is finished, and the homeowner rarely connects the two. If a post oak on your lot has thinning crown and short annual growth, [deep root fertilization](/services/tree-health/deep-root-fertilization/) and soil decompaction are the first move, not removal.

## Do you work along Spring Creek?

Yes, and those trees get looked at before anything is cut. Spring Creek runs along the south edge of the Tomball area, and Spring Creek Park covers 114 acres of that corridor. Bank trees there sit on undercut soil, and a tree that looks sound from the yard can have half its root plate hanging over open water.

Creek bank removals are rigged, not felled, for that reason. The risk is not the cutting, it is the root plate lifting when weight shifts.

## Do you clear acreage on the Montgomery County side?

Yes. North and west of Tomball toward Pinehurst and Decker Prairie the lots open into acreage, horse property, and wooded tracts, and the work becomes [lot clearing](/services/land-clearing/lot-clearing/) and fence line clearing rather than single tree work.

Before any digging on those jobs, underground utilities have to be marked. [Texas 811](https://www.texas811.org/) locates them at no cost, and no machine goes in the ground until the site is flagged.
