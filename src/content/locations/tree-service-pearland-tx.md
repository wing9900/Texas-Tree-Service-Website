Pearland has grown faster than almost any city on the south side of Houston, and its trees tell the story twice. The Old Townsite streets have mature oaks and pecans that need real arborist care, while the newer subdivisions off Shadow Creek Parkway have builder-planted trees now big enough to cause their first problems. Silverlake and Green Tee Terrace sit in between, old enough that the first round of plantings has reached full size. We cover all of it from our Pasadena shop, about half an hour up Beltway 8, with an ISA Certified Arborist on every job.

## What tree problems come with Pearland's clay soil?

Roots, mostly. Pearland yards sit on expansive clay that swells and shrinks with the rain, and thirsty roots follow the moisture straight under driveways, patios, and foundation slabs. It gets worse the closer you are to drainage: the ground along the Mary's Creek trail at Centennial Park stays damp long after a yard two streets away has dried out, and roots read that difference before you do. If a tree was planted too close to concrete, a [root barrier installation](/services/tree-health/root-barrier-installation/) redirects the roots down and away before the slab pays for it. Where the damage argument is already lost, [root removal](/services/stump-grinding/root-removal/) takes out the surface roots that are doing the lifting.

## Do you clear lots for new construction in Pearland?

Yes, and Pearland still has plenty of wooded parcels going under new homes and shops. Our [lot clearing](/services/land-clearing/lot-clearing/) crews take out the trees, grind or extract the stumps, and haul the debris so the pad is genuinely ready. The mixed hardwood and brush along the Shadow Creek Ranch Nature Trail is a good likeness of what a lot of these raw parcels look like before anything is cleared. Texas A&M Forest Service, the state's forestry agency, has good guidance on which trees are worth designing a build around at [tfsweb.tamu.edu](https://tfsweb.tamu.edu/).

## Does Pearland require approval before a tree comes down?

Ask before you cut, because Pearland is the strictest city in our service area on this point. The city's own Community Development guidance states that under Unified Development Code Section 4.2.3.3, no person may cut down, remove, or destroy a tree within city limits without first submitting a tree survey and obtaining approval through Urban Forestry. That language reads broader than what the neighboring cities publish, and Pearland is the one place we tell homeowners to confirm with the city before the job goes on the calendar.

Deed restrictions are a separate layer that state law leaves alone, so in master-planned communities like Shadow Creek Ranch the HOA rules can be tighter than the city's. We sort out both at the estimate: Robert identifies and measures the tree properly, then points you to the right desk. The city posts the code and the current process at [pearlandtx.gov](https://www.pearlandtx.gov/). None of this is legal advice, the code does get amended, and we ask the survey question fresh on every Pearland job.
