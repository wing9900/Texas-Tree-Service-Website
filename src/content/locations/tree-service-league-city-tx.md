## Who should prune a mature live oak in League City?

Someone working to the ANSI A300 standard, because a bad prune is permanent. That means cuts landing at the branch collar, no climbing spikes in a tree that stays, and conservative canopy removal instead of heavy thinning. ## Can a storm-worried League City homeowner shrink a tree without topping it?

Yes. ## Does League City require a permit to remove a tree?

Not on your own single-family home, which surprises most people who have heard the city has a tree ordinance. League City does run one of the strongest tree preservation codes in the area, administered by a City Arborist, and it does require a Tree Disposition Permit for protected trees. The exemption that covers most homeowners is written into it: the owner of a legally conforming single-family residence may remove a tree on that property without a permit and without mitigation. Commercial lots, new development, and trees tied to an approved landscape plan are the ones that need paperwork.

Two details are worth knowing before anyone cuts. Healthy oaks and pecans measuring 38 inches or more in caliper are treated as significant trees, and the city will not permit removing one except under narrow findings. And a tree posing an immediate danger can come down without waiting on a permit, but you have to be able to substantiate the emergency afterward, so photograph the lean, the split, or the failure before a saw touches it. We document every League City emergency job that way as a matter of course.

The code was amended as recently as late 2025, and the city posts the current version and permit process at [leaguecitytx.gov](https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/1825/Tree-Removal-Permit).
